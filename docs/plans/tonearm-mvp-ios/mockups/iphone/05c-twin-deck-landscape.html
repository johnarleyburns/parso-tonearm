§42.7a Twin deck — landscape. TwinDeckView ▸ WorkspaceModel ▸ PerformanceEngine, TelemetryPump. Pro. A 6-inch screen cannot hold two decks, a mixer and four stem faders at a usable touch size (§42.1). It does not have to: the binding constraint on a phone is reach, not pixels. Held in two hands in landscape, the screen divides into two ~90 pt thumb arcs at the bottom corners and a large centre region no thumb can get to without regripping. So the centre carries only display — parallel waveforms on one shared playhead, master spectrum, beat phase — and every control sits in an arc: a 168 pt jog per deck (≈28 mm, matched to the sweep a thumb makes without regripping), CUE/PLAY/LOOP at 54 pt on the inner side, the crossfader on the bottom centre edge where either thumb reaches it, and filter as a slider on each screen edge, which costs no layout width at all. The four control banks — EQ, stems, pads, cues — are not on this screen; they rise as momentary drawers over one jog (§42.7b), because you never need a jog and an EQ in the same instant. The jog's white marker is track position; the cyan ghost marker is the other deck's beat phase, so aligning two dots is beatmatching without headphones — an affordance hardware cannot offer. The two 59 pt bands are the landscape sensor-housing inset, mirrored; nothing interactive may live there. Thermal state, granted buffer and latency (FR-SESS-2) and record state keep their top strip, per the mockup conventions.
