§42.7b The momentary bank drawer. This is the screen that proves the twin-deck model, the way 05b proves the solo-deck one. A phone genuinely cannot show two jogs and two EQ sections and stem faders at 44 pt — the arithmetic is unarguable: three 44 pt knobs plus gaps is 152 pt, which fits one 228 pt deck column and cannot fit two side by side in the 202 pt centre. So the banks are momentary, not resident: hold the bank tab and the drawer rises over that deck's jog — 228 pt of the 734, covering nothing else. Both waveforms, both transports, deck B's jog, the beat-phase meter and the crossfader all stay live and visible; §42.7's rule that a sheet may never cover the crossfader holds here unchanged. Spring-loading is the point: a held drawer cannot leave the surface in a mode the user has forgotten about, which is the failure that makes small DJ UIs dangerous mid-set. A tap pins it for hands-free use and it self-dismisses after 12 s. Filter is deliberately not in the drawer — it stays on the screen edge, still visible and still under a thumb with the drawer open, because filter and EQ get worked together. Deck A's jog and transport are occluded, and only they. The limiter has gone amber and the phase meter shows B drifting +6.4 ms: the two readouts that tell a DJ to reach for the jog, both outside the drawer.
